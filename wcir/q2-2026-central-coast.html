What this quarter surfaced

The region is roughly half-visible

When a traveler asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, or Gemini where to taste on the Central Coast, only some wineries get named. We put a fixed panel of real trip-planning questions to all four AI assistants and recorded which tasting rooms came back.

In Santa Barbara County, 100 of 171 tasting rooms were named by at least one AI assistant. In San Luis Obispo, 95 of 252. Put the other way, 228 tasting rooms across the two counties do not come up at all when a traveler asks an AI assistant where to taste.

That is not a small marketing gap. It is a channel much of the region is absent from, in front of travelers who are planning trips there.

The finding underneath the finding

Being named once is not the same as being findable

The figures above count every tasting room named by any of the four AI assistants. The comparison that follows holds Gemini out, because Gemini was not in the field last quarter and counting it on only one side would credit the region for a change we made to our own instrument. On that narrower basis, a quarter ago we ran the same panel three times in the same week and got 73, 76 and 77 Santa Barbara tasting rooms cited. Nothing about those tasting rooms changed in between. This quarter's single pass returned 78, measured against 77, the strongest of those three earlier passes, so the gain is stated against April's best rather than its average.

That spread is worth more than the gain. It means a tasting room near the edge of visibility drifts in and out of these answers on its own. Any measurement of this channel that reports a single number without a spread is quietly overstating its own precision. That includes this quarter's own 78, which is why April's three passes are printed next to it.

Twelve tasting rooms came up this quarter that came up in none of those earlier passes, which is a harder test than it sounds. They had three chances before and took none, then one chance now and took it.

There is a second gap worth naming, and it is the one most likely to catch an owner off guard. Ahrefs put 15,000 prompts to ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity, a slightly different panel than ours, and checked how often the pages each one cited also ranked in Google's top ten for the same question. Perplexity was the outlier at 28.6%. The others sat near 8%. The measurement is young, but ranking well does not buy you a citation: a tasting room can rank well in search and still not be named when a traveler asks where to taste.

Method

What we deliberately did not claim

We had a quarter-over-quarter improvement figure for Santa Barbara that was larger and better-looking than the one in the report, and we threw it out. Our first pass compared this quarter against last quarter without accounting for the fact that we had asked each question three times in the earlier round and once in this one, and had added a fourth AI assistant in between. Most of the apparent gain was our own measurement changing, not the region improving.

We also do not report score-level or rank movement this quarter, because the earlier edition was scored under a previous version of our rubric, and a change in the ruler would read as a change in the business.

We would rather hand you a smaller number you can defend in public than a larger one you cannot.
