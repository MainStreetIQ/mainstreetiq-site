The eight core questions

1. Do you use more than one bank?

2. Are operating, payroll, tax-reserve, and savings dollars held in separate accounts?

3. Without logging into a bank, can you state your current total cash position right now?

4. Do you have a documented minimum operating balance, with an alert when you cross it?

5. Do you have active credit relationships with more than one lender or institution?

6. Could you secure new credit today, on terms you would accept, without changing your business position?

7. Do you maintain a rolling 13-week cash forecast, updated at least monthly?

8. Do you have a documented way of collecting what you are owed, covering when invoices go out, when reminders fire, and when you escalate?

    What a strong stack looks like

    A resilient stack is not complicated, it is just deliberate. Banking keeps a second relationship and separate accounts so tax money is never spent by accident. Treasury means the cash position is visible at a glance. Float is knowing how long cash takes to come back after it goes out. Credit architecture is having more than one lender who already knows the business. And a liquidity system is a simple rolling forecast that turns next quarter from a guess into a plan. None of these are customer-facing, which is why they get postponed. They are also among the most fixable findings a business can act on.
