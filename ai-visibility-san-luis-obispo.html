Why this matters now

Discovery moved, and most wineries did not

A visitor used to find you through a ranked page of links and a map of pins. They compared, they clicked, they decided. That funnel is still there, but a new one now sits in front of it: the visitor asks an AI which wineries to visit, reads one synthesized answer, and often stops there.

San Luis Obispo County has hundreds of tasting rooms, from Paso Robles and Templeton to the Edna Valley and the Arroyo Grande Valley, and a visitor has to find one before they can choose it. When the search that used to surface a map of pins now returns a handful of names, being one of them is the whole game.

That answer is not built from your website alone. The model assembles it from your reviews, your directory listings, the articles that mention you, the schema on your pages, and the wider citation footprint that surrounds your name. If those signals are thin or stale, you are absent from the answer no matter how good the wine is. Often the winery that gets named is not winning because it is better; it is winning because it is legible to the machine and you are not.

How the engine works

Find, prioritize, track

Three moves that show you where you stand, what to fix first, and whether it is working.

1. Find every touchpoint

We map the full set of places a visitor to San Luis Obispo County can discover you and score each one. You leave knowing where you show up, where you are absent, and which gaps are costing you the most, instead of guessing which channel to chase this quarter.

2. Prioritize what actually moves visitors

Not every fix is worth making. We rank the fixes by what actually pulls visitors toward you, from your reviews and listings to the pages you control, so your team's effort lands where it compounds rather than where it is convenient.

3. Track it as it drifts

The models change, competitors move, and a footprint left alone goes stale. Monitor is the outside check that the work is still working: it re-reads your visibility every month and flags the drift, so you can see whether the gains from month one are holding in month twelve.

The outcome we are after is simple: more of the visitors already planning a trip end up in your tasting room. How far that moves depends on your starting point and your own execution; we measure it against your real numbers rather than promise a figure.
