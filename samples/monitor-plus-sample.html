How to read this report

These explanations are the same every month, so they sit here rather than in the body. Anything that changed this month is said where it applies.

You and your peer set. The peer-set table scores every winery the same way on the same day, out of the same points across the same categories. Its last column is how often the AI assistants named each winery when asked about wineries in your county that month. Each category column shows points earned; its maximum is in the column heading. Rank in county counts only wineries with enough measures read to publish a score.

Since last month. The since-last-month table covers your Google reviews, rating and listing checks. A measure is compared only where we read it the same way both months, on the same Google listing. Typical is the middle value. A change of one or two reviews in a month is ordinary.

Drift Watch. Five things we watch every month, and whether any of them moved against you since last month. An alert fires only on a change we measured both months. How often AI assistants name you is kept out of the score. It is the result we are all working toward, so we measure it on its own, in the AI section of this report.

The 7 categories, one by one. Third-Party Presence: do the lists, guides, and press that visitors and AI assistants read name you? Review Volume & Reputation: how many people vouch for you, and are they still doing it? Search & Map Discovery: when a visitor searches for wineries rather than for you by name, do you come up, in the results and on the map? AI-Ready Booking & Buying: once someone has picked you, can they book, join, and buy without picking up the phone? Profile Accuracy: does your Google profile state the right facts, and does Apple Maps agree? Citation Readiness: when a search engine or AI assistant reads your site, can it get the facts out? Social: does your Instagram confirm you're open, active, and worth visiting? Each category's link to being named by AI assistants, across every winery we score, is in the appendix table of category links. A link like this is a pattern across wineries, not proof of cause; the months ahead will show whether raising a score brings more mentions.

Your AI questions. An answer is one assistant's reply to one question in one round of asking. Named means your winery appears anywhere in the reply. A question counts as won when you are named in at least half of its answers. The table by assistant shows how often each assistant named you, the wineries it named most and the sources it cited most. Counts are answers (an answer naming a winery or citing a site counts once). Your own site is included among the sources. A question marked (priority) is one of your priority questions. A question can favor other wineries by where it is set or by what it asks for. A question is listed here when you were named in a quarter of its answers or fewer, one other winery was named in at least half, and the question names a place your tasting room is not in or asks for something we do not have on file as an offer of yours.

The citation gap: what the assistants cited instead. For each priority question, the sources the assistants cited most. Where you are not winning, we read the answers that named other wineries and not you; where you are, the answers that named you. What we measured is which addresses were cited; whether a source mentions you on a page we did not see is not measured.

Where the points are. Every measure where a peer scores ahead of you, ranked by what closing the gap is worth and by how strongly that measure goes with being named by AI assistants. Where the fix lives: a fix on your site moves in weeks; on a platform profile, in days; off your site (reviews, press, lists), it takes a campaign over months. The column headed This month says whether a gap is one of this month's moves, last month's move still open, or not advised; a blank is a gap ranked below this month's moves. Yelp asks businesses not to request reviews; that count tends to follow guest volume and a complete listing rather than asking.

Last month's moves. Each move is checked against this month's reading; movement is reported as what happened after the move was sent, never as its cause.

Basics check. This lists only what broke, changed or could not be read since last month, checked the same way both months.

Appendix: method and category links. A winery is named when we find its name, an alias or its domain in the reply. Shares are answers naming you over all answers to that question, every assistant pooled. AI answers vary from round to round. The spread is the gap between the highest and lowest round. A month-on-month move smaller than the spread is reported as no clear change. The sources are the addresses each assistant returned with its answer. For each category and measure, the link to AI naming is how closely the points a winery earns rank with the share of its own county's AI answers that name it, across every winery we score, graded strong, moderate, weak or none.
