The new discovery channel

45 of 442 wineries account for 47.5% of every time AI names a winery

The report asks six AI assistants the trip-planning questions a visitor would ask about each county, three times over the month, and counts which wineries they name: 2,644 answers in Q3 2026, naming a winery 23,432 times. Most wineries are named at least once for their own county's questions, 388 of 442, but a small group gets nearly half of the namings. The ten named most often account for 17.4% of every naming, and 54 wineries were never named in an answer to their own county's questions.

A winery with strong Google rankings can be completely absent from AI results. The signals are different, and the wineries winning here are not always the ones winning in traditional search.

About as many wineries were named overall; what changed is which ones. In the set of questions and assistants we kept unchanged from Q2 2026, the 40 wineries named less often went from 343 namings to 225, and 99 of those 118 fewer namings came from the two assistants in that set that search the web for every answer. That points to a change in what those searches now find, not necessarily at the wineries.

And travelers are asking: MMGY Travel Intelligence's summer 2026 survey of 4,371 U.S. adults found that 52% of active leisure travelers have used AI to help plan a trip, up 10 points in a year.

Running the identical panel more than once returns a different set of wineries, with nothing about those wineries changing in between. That is why the report asks each question three times over the month. A winery near the edge of visibility drifts in and out of these answers on its own.

The methodology

Seven categories, scored from public data

Every winery is scored on the same seven categories, 100 points in total, drawn entirely from publicly accessible sources, the same places a visitor looks. No surveys, no self-reporting, no private analytics.

Third-Party Presence

Do the lists, guides, and press that visitors and AI assistants read name you?

Review Volume & Reputation

How many people vouch for you, and are they still doing it?

Search & Map Discovery

When a visitor searches for wineries rather than for you by name, do you come up, in the results and on the map?

AI-Ready Booking & Buying

Once someone has picked you, can they book, join, and buy without picking up the phone?

Profile Accuracy

Does your Google profile state the right facts, and does Apple Maps agree?

Citation Readiness

When a search engine or AI assistant reads your site, can it get the facts out?

Social

Does your Instagram confirm you're open, active, and worth visiting?

From the founder

Why a Finance Firm Publishes a Wine Report

I am Scott Hess, founder of Main Street IQ. We work in two ways: Fractional CFO, and Wine Country Intelligence, our AI advisory for wineries. The obvious thing for a finance firm to publish would be a finance report. This is not that, and the reason is the whole point.

Strip the question down to what a winery owner cannot already see. Not the P&L; you have someone for that. The blind spot is whether you show up when a traveler asks an AI trip planner or a search engine where to taste, when a buyer compares you against the tasting room down the road, when your name should come up and quietly does not.

Through Wine Country Intelligence, I measure visibility for the same reason a CFO measures club attrition: both sit upstream of your margin. Visibility decides whether you get found. Getting found decides what a new customer costs you. What a customer costs decides whether the tasting room, the club, and the wholesale book actually make money. The report reads the top of that chain, further from the ledger than most finance work ever looks.

Santa Barbara County's wine industry generates $1.7 billion in annual economic activity and draws 1.1 million visitors a year. Those visitors increasingly decide where to taste before they leave home, checking reviews, scanning Instagram, and asking AI assistants for recommendations. Great wine has never been enough on its own; the report shows every winery in the county exactly where it stands on the surfaces that decision runs through.

It is also the lightest way to work with us: intelligence that sharpens decisions you are already making, with you in the driver's seat. Some owners stop there. Some bring Wine Country Intelligence in to fix what the report finds. Some bring our Fractional CFO work into the pricing, club, and allocation decisions. Either way, it starts with a number you could not see before.

Scott Hess | Founder and Fractional CFO, Main Street IQ | scott@mainstreetiq.com
