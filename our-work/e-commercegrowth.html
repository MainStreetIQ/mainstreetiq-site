The Challenge

Watch Gang was growing fast, and the finance function had not kept pace. Acquisition was scaling, the subscriber base was compounding, and the same processes that worked at an earlier stage were buckling under the volume. Inventory decisions were made on gut feel. Nobody could say with confidence which acquisition channels were actually profitable once retention and churn were factored in. The CEO was making major capital decisions without real-time visibility into what the business was actually doing.

The gap between revenue growth and financial infrastructure was widening every quarter.

What We Built

Acquisition Economics by Cohort

We rebuilt CAC and LTV at the cohort and channel level, so spend could be judged on whether it actually paid back rather than on top-line subscriber adds. Recalibrating channel mix against real cohort math brought acquisition cost down roughly 5% without slowing growth, and retention improvements added about 7 points to the base that compounds every month.

Inventory & Cash Flow Integration

We tied inventory forecasting models directly to cash flow projections, giving the team visibility into the working capital impact of every purchase decision before it was made. No more buying on intuition: every order was backed by data and mapped to a cash flow timeline.

Margin Analysis

We implemented contribution margin tracking by product line and acquisition source. For the first time, leadership could see exactly where margin was generated and where it was surrendered, down to the SKU and the channel.

KPI Dashboard & Faster Close

We replaced manual weekly reports with a unified dashboard tracking the metrics that actually matter (CAC, LTV, inventory turns, fulfillment cost) and tightened the monthly close by roughly 40%. Real-time visibility replaced the multi-week reporting lag that had been the norm, so decisions were made on this month's numbers, not last quarter's.

Strategic Finance

Beyond the operational work, we provided guidance on the decisions that shaped the next phase of growth: warehouse versus 3PL economics with the hidden costs included, credit facility structuring for inventory financing, and migration off entry-level bookkeeping onto an enterprise-grade financial system. The combination grew ARR about 22% while taking roughly 12% out of operating expense.

Engagement Model

Embedded as CFO, not advising from the outside. Weekly in the business, owning the finance function end to end, part of the team making the calls.
